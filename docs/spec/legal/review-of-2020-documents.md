# Review of the 2020 documents

Oct 4, 2026 · @Tesfaye Beyene

The Terms and Conditions and the Privacy Policy on ethio.com today, both dated 14 December 2020, were written for an online shop with payments, subscriptions and advertising trackers. The new ethio.com is none of those, so most of their text is dropped. The rules that still apply are now in the drafts, and the review exposed one inaccurate line in the draft Privacy Policy, which is corrected.

## What other marketplaces say

All four marketplaces read on 4 October keep the right to remove ads and close accounts without notice, and all four disclaim the ads. Terms 5 and 12 now do the same.

| Marketplace | Removing ads and closing accounts | Responsibility for ads and deals | Liability limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Craigslist](https://www.craigslist.org/about/terms.of.use) | Moderates at its sole discretion: blocking, deleting, terminating accounts. No notice is promised. | Disclaims responsibility for the conduct of any user. | 100 dollars, or the fees paid in the past year |
| [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/user-agreement?id=4259) | May end the service to anyone, for any reason, at its discretion. | No control over, and no guarantee of, the items, the listings or the sellers. | 100 dollars, or more under its own guarantee |
| [OfferUp](https://offerup.com/terms) | May remove content at any time, for any reason, with or without notice. | Not a party to any deal. No duty to monitor. Its badge is not proof of identity. | The greater of 100 dollars or 12 months of fees |
| [Jiji Ethiopia](https://jiji.com.et/rules.html) | May suspend or close an account with or without notice, and delete ads without notice or explanation. | Not a party to any deal. No control over, and no guarantee of, the goods. | The lesser of 6 months of fees or 3,400 birr |
| ethio.com draft | May remove ads and restrict or close accounts at any time, with or without notice, for any reason (Terms 5). | A venue only. Nothing is verified. Users deal at their own risk (Terms 1 and 12). | 100 dollars |

Two clauses come from single sites. Craigslist says it is not liable for moderating or for not moderating. eBay and OfferUp have users release the company from disputes between users. The draft now has both.

In the United States this wording holds. A federal law, Section 230, also protects a site from liability for what its users post and for removing it in good faith. In the EU and the UK it holds less. A platform there must tell a user why an ad or account was restricted, and a term that lets a company end a consumer's contract without notice can be struck as unfair. Terms 5 therefore keeps the reasons and the appeal as practice, and says the law of the user's country applies where it requires more.

## What is left

Your answers of 4 October settled every point. The record is in rows 13 to 16 of the table in Read me first. One action waits for the launch checklist: registering the copyright agent.

1. **Gambling, betting and lotteries.** Banned. The line stays in Terms 4, and a screening rule follows.
2. **Notice period.** 30 days, as your current Terms promise. It is a setting.
3. **Copyright agent.** No phone number is printed in the Terms, as on Craigslist, eBay and OfferUp. The agent must be registered with the United States Copyright Office before launch: 6 dollars, renewed every three years ([DMCA designated agent directory](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/)). The registration asks for a phone number, so a separate number that goes to voicemail is used there.
4. **Acting without notice, and the disclaimer.** Added to Terms 5 and 12, in the wording the four marketplaces above use.

The lawyer's list on the first tab gains five points: which countries the sanctions line closes, how prominently the liability limit must be printed, whether the California privacy law applies, whether the agent's phone number must be printed on the site, and how far the right to act without notice holds in the EU and the UK.

The two 2020 pages come down on the day the new site replaces the old one. If old accounts are carried over, each is asked to accept version 1 at its first sign-in.
