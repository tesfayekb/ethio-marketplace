Good — that confirms D62-2 is live. On discounts: I looked at how the big platforms do it, because the design choice here is mostly about **honesty of the "was" price**, not UI.

**What others do**
- **eBay (Sale Event + Markdown):** percentage or fixed amount off; the item must have been listed at the pre-sale price for **at least 14 days** before a strikethrough may be shown; a sale runs **1–14 days**, then the item is excluded from sales for 14 days; buyers see before/after prices with the original struck through; editing the price during a sale drops it out of the sale ([eBay UK Seller Centre](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/grow-your-sales/using-promotions-to-boost-your-sales/sale-event-markdown), [eBay Discounts Manager](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/discounts-manager?id=4094)).
- **Etsy:** percentage off only, **5–75%**, whole numbers; when several sales overlap, only the highest discount applies; sellers are told they are responsible for consumer-protection law on promotions ([Etsy Help — Sales and Discounts](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115014260108-How-to-Set-Up-Sales-and-Discounts-for-Your-Shop)).
- **Facebook Marketplace:** no reliable "price drop" badge or crossed-out price; sellers write "Was X — Now Y" into the title themselves ([pocketmoney.co.nz guide](https://pocketmoney.co.nz/resources/how-to-mark-down-price-on-facebook-marketplace-nz-step-by-step)). Vinted and Depop take the other route: when a seller **lowers the price**, the old price is shown struck through and watchers are notified — no seller-typed "original price" at all.

The lesson: never let a seller **type** the "was" price. A typed reference price is the classic fake-discount pattern and is what consumer-protection rules target; on eBay the "was" is the price the item actually carried for 14 days, on Vinted it is the price history. Both are honest by construction.

**Proposal — D68 "Price drops & sales"** (two layers, both computed, no cron):

1. **Automatic price drop (your "didn't sell, comes back cheaper" case).** The door keeps a price history row on every price change of a published listing. When the current price is lower than the price the listing carried for at least 7 days, the card and detail show the old price struck through and a "Price reduced −12%" ribbon for 30 days. Zero seller input, cannot be faked by raising-then-lowering within the week. Watchers/favouriters get the notification later (D56 recents/favourites).
2. **Declared sale.** On the price step (and in My Listings → "Put on sale"): a **Discount** disclosure → amount or percentage (5–75%, whole numbers), start (today by default), end: presets **1 week · 2 weeks · 1 month · until sold · custom date · until an event** (Meskel, Timket, Genna, Fasika, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Enkutatash — dates from a small table we maintain yearly). The regular price stays the regular price; the sale price is derived. The "was" strikethrough is shown only if the regular price has been live ≥ 7 days; otherwise the ribbon reads "Sale −10%" without a "was". Sale length capped at 30 days, 7-day cooldown before the next sale on the same listing. When the end passes, the effective price reverts by computation — no job to run, nothing to expire.

Data: regular `price_amount` unchanged; `sale_amount`/`sale_pct`, `sale_starts_at`, `sale_ends_at`, `sale_label`; `listing_price_history`; an `effective_price` function used by cards, detail, sorting and the price filter. Rules live in the door (D31 pattern); percent math server-side (minor units, no floats).
