**1. Gambling, betting and lotteries:** banned. The line stays in Terms 4 and goes onto the project's banned list as a screening rule.

**2. The phone number:** you don't have to post one, and I removed it. US copyright law protects a site from claims over photos its users post, but only if the site names a "copyright agent" to receive complaints. The law lists a phone number among the agent's details. Craigslist, eBay and OfferUp print no phone on their terms pages, so Terms 15 now shows only the address and legal@ethio.com. The registration form itself does ask for a phone; when we get there, we use a separate number that only takes voicemail, not yours.

**3. Registration:** noted as a launch-checklist item. I'll walk you through it step by step then.

**4. Acting without notice:** yes, it is best for you, and it is now in Terms 5. All four marketplaces I read today do it:

| Marketplace | Removing ads and closing accounts | Liability limit |
|---|---|---|
| Craigslist | Sole discretion; no notice promised | 100 dollars or the past year's fees |
| eBay | Anyone, any reason, at its discretion | 100 dollars |
| OfferUp | Any time, any reason, with or without notice | 100 dollars or 12 months of fees |
| Jiji Ethiopia | With or without notice; ads deleted without explanation | 6 months of fees or 3,400 birr |

Terms 5 now says:
- We may remove or hide any ad, and restrict, suspend or close any account, at any time, with or without notice, for any reason.
- We have no duty to screen, and are not liable for acting or not acting.
- The steps, reasons and appeal are our practice, not a promise.
- Where a country's law requires a reason or notice, we give it.

That last line matters. In the US the wording holds. In the EU and UK a platform must tell a user why it acted, and a no-notice term against a consumer can be struck as unfair. So the steps you approved stay, as practice.

**5. The disclaimer:** Terms 12 is rewritten and renamed "Your responsibility and our liability". It now says:
- Every ad comes from the user who posted it; we do not verify, endorse or guarantee it.
- We do not verify identity, ownership, quality, safety or legality, and passing our screening proves none of these.
- Users deal at their own risk and must check for themselves before paying.
- We are not a party to any deal and not liable for loss, injury or dispute.
- Users release ethio.com from disputes between users (as eBay and OfferUp do).

**Two additions I made to the build plan**, because strong words only help if the user saw them:
- The acceptance screen shows three short lines above the tick: we do not verify ads, you deal at your own risk, we may remove ads or close accounts without notice.
- Every ad carries a fixed line beside the seller's contact: "ethio.com does not verify sellers or ads. Inspect before you pay."

No wording covers everything. It does not protect us if we are told about an illegal ad and leave it up, or against copyright claims until the agent is registered. I'm not a lawyer; the EU and UK question is on the lawyer's list.
